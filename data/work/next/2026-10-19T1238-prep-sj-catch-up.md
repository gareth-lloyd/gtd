Catch-up with SJ on Wednesday. What SJ cares most about, from his remarks at the EPD All Hands on 16 Oct 2026 (transcript: ~/Downloads/GMT20261016-152347_Recording.transcript.vtt; recording: https://canarytechnologies.slack.com/archives/C05K3SM1EDC/p1792179723676979).

- **Turning signed deals into live revenue.** He said the focus is flipping to about 65/35 live revenue over signed contracts. His test for any work: "Is this serving ARR or CARR?"
  - Color: Q3 bookings beat plan (enterprise $23.6M vs $19.9M) but activations missed badly (enterprise $1.65M vs $9.41M, commercial $4.03M vs $7.12M). He called bookings "THE leading indicator", but said the board weighting has moved from 100/0 CARR five years ago to about 50/50 or 60/40 last year: "we need to flip the focus onto ARR... your answer should be different than last year." The $48.5M CARR-to-ARR backlog is 54% IHG, 28% Wyndham Voice AI and $8.6M "everyone else".

- **Rebuilding implementation, not polishing it.** "Build the car, don't build the faster horse." Only 27% of Q3 bookings went live in the same quarter.
  - Color: "Re-underwrite everything we're doing on implementations... do not be iterative." The ask was aimed at Stephanie and team. His example was the Zendesk support agent: "we all clapped... but that was a faster horse." The support agent should offer "would you like me to make this change for you?" and then make it. He framed it as making it easy for sales and CS to take hotels live with one click.

- **Saying no and cutting what exists.** He wants nominations for things to cut.
  - Color: This came from Jordan Sterling saying products are spread across 2-3 engineers each. SJ: "We shouldn't just maintain everything we've shipped just because we shipped it... flag them." He named Tablet Reg in new international countries, the long tail of tipping and HR integrations, and new countries as cuts. He admits he's better at adding: "I'm not good at this column... lobby for adding things to this column." He listed past refusals (native brand apps, China data residency) that the team never hears about. Blake added that deprioritising can mean leaving a product "shelf-ready" rather than turning it off.
  - Mobile angle: Laura Viglioni asked whether the Tablet Reg country rules also apply to mobile check-in. SJ: "Yes."

- **Speeding up EMEA go-lives.** Half of the $8.6M "everyone else" backlog is EMEA.
  - Color: He said the EMEA pod was the fix for last year's model, where one product person relayed EMEA requirements to other pods. He also blamed "Europeans are indecisive" (they now sign with future go-live dates and opt-outs) and "on vacation all the damn time". He sees EMEA ARR as growth: "if you don't take it to ARR, then they don't get value. If they don't get value, they don't tell other groups." James Saram: EMEA customers expect one platform for everything, which means more integrations; pilots run 60-90 days and need both sides committed.

- **Getting the big enterprise rollouts right.** IHG should be predictable; Wyndham Voice AI is the largest activation gap.
  - Color: IHG is GMS, so there is nothing new to build and it should be "a very predictable deployment". He wants EPD to make it "one-click" for the team; Connor added that Canary controls IHG sign-ups. For Wyndham Voice AI, the AI closes fewer bookings than human agents. The proposal moves from 70% fully AI-handled calls to 50%, plus an agent-assist tool to halve handle time on the rest. The 70% target was "kind of made up". He says confidence is "still very high".

Possible angle for Wednesday: Mobile could offer a concrete cut or scope boundary tied to EMEA go-lives, using the same country-rules point that applies to Tablet Reg.
